# Bringing a character in

A character you already play somewhere else can come to the table from its
sheet. ThunderForge reads a PDF of the sheet, shows you what it found, and
writes nothing until you accept.

D&D 5e reads the PDF that D&D Beyond exports. Roll for Shoes reads its own
sheet. Other game systems do not take sheets yet, and do not offer the
button.

## Bringing a sheet in

You need a character you may edit: one you have claimed, or one your Game
Master made for you to fill in.

1. Open the character, or find your card on the world's **Players** screen.
2. Press **Bring in a sheet**.
3. Choose the PDF. It is read in your browser first; nothing is sent yet.

A sheet is refused when it is password-protected, larger than 10 MB, longer
than 20 pages, or not a character sheet this game system can read. The
page says which.

## Reading the review

The review lists every value the sheet will write, beside what the
character holds now.

- **Read**: taken plainly from the sheet.
- **Check this**: read, but something about it looked doubtful. The reason
  is shown. Correct the value if it is wrong; the field is then marked as
  corrected, and your Game Master can see that you did.
- **Not read**: not on the sheet, or not readable. It is never written, and
  what the character has stays.

Use the filters at the top to show only what needs checking or what was not
read.

**Cross-checks** compare what the sheet states with what follows from the
rest of it, such as a modifier against its score. A mismatch is shown so
you can look; it does not stop you.

Values the game system has no place for go into the character's notes,
under "From the imported sheet".

Press **Accept** to bring the character in, or **Decline** to leave without
changing anything.

## Spells, features and items

Spells, feats, features and items on the sheet are matched against what your
world already has.

- One the world has is linked to the world's own.
- One the world does not have is brought in and waits for the Game Master.
  On the character it shows as **awaiting the GM**, and it cannot be used at
  the table until it is adopted.
- One the Game Master turned down shows as **declined by the GM**. It stays
  off the table.

Trying to use something that has not been adopted is refused, and your Game
Master is told what was tried, so they can decide on it.

## For the Game Master: "Brought by players"

The Game Master and Trusted Players see a **Brought by players** tab in the
world's compendium. Each player's pieces are grouped together.

- **Adopt** puts a piece into the world's compendium. Every character that
  brought it then uses the world's.
- **Decline** keeps it off the table.
- **Revisit** reopens a declined piece.
- **Adopt all from** a player adopts everything they brought that is still
  waiting. Anything they bring afterwards waits as usual.

Two players whose sheets describe the same piece differently each have
their own row, marked as differing, so you can choose which to adopt.

## Bringing a character in again

After a level-up, bring the new sheet in the same way. The review then shows
only what changes. What the table tracks in play, such as current hit points
or spell slots used, is kept unless you tick it to be overwritten.

## Rolling back

The character's page lists every sheet brought in, newest first. The Game
Master can press **Roll back to before this** on any of them. The sheet's
values go back to what they were; what the table tracks in play stays.
The roll back is listed in the history too. Players cannot roll back.

## Where the sheet is kept

Each sheet you bring in is kept on the server, so the history can say what
was read and from what.

- You and the Game Master can press **Download sheet** in the history.
  Nobody else at the table can.
- The sheets are in your account's data export.
- Deleting your account deletes the sheets you brought in. The history keeps
  its rows, marked "file no longer kept".
